# Commodore 64: MOS 6581 SID (`c64`)

The Commodore 64's sound: the 6581, three voices that are each an oscillator
with four waveforms that can be combined, an envelope with a gate, sync and
ring modulation between neighbours, and a filter they share. The method
behind every section is in [CONFORMANCE.md](../CONFORMANCE.md).

**What the numbers say.** Every log is identical to reSID-fp on all six
streams: every waveform at every pitch and pulse width in the corpus, the four
combined waveforms and the noise combinations with their write-back into the
register, the noise at eight rates and its reset by the test bit, every
attack, decay and release rate, every sustain level, gates a few cycles apart
in every phase, the rate register set below its count mid-attack, sync and
ring modulation at four ratios including a synced source, the test bit on a
source, a source at zero, and the two songs through the driver. Two things the
first run found, both in the chip and both facts about the hardware rather
than the model: power-on is a reset, which clocks the noise register once as
the reset line goes; and rate 8 of the envelope is 392 cycles a step, one
more than the datasheet's 391, which is what the register value kevtris read
off the chip says. The combined waveforms are a model with six numbers per
combination, and the model matches reSID-fp's tables on all 4096 entries of
each: `pnpm --filter chipvoice-conform fit:c64` scores it.

## Analog stage

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Reference unit | none yet |
| Capture | none |
| Tolerance | |
| Maximum band error | unmeasured |
| Corners measured | none |
| Resampling | one host sample per PAL-clock period, the cycles averaged; the output stage after that |

The stage is a profile from the documents, `SID_6581_PROFILE`: the two DACs
per voice as R-2R ladders with a 2R/R mismatch of 2.2 and no termination,
which is what makes the 6581's DACs non-linear; the waveform DAC's zero at
0x380, which is why the 6581's envelopes click; a two-integrator state-variable
filter whose cutoff follows the curve reSID measured on one 6581 R4AR, kink at
1024 included, with Q from 0.707 to 1.707; the output stage's corners at 16 Hz
and 16 kHz. None of it has been measured on a unit of our own, and 6581s
differ from each other more than any other chip here; the 8580, with its
linear DACs and its own filter, is not modelled. A capture of a real unit's
line-out under a known script is what it needs (P7-8).

## Driver coverage

`SidDriver` in `packages/chipvoice/src/chips/c64/driver.ts`, checked by
`test/c64-driver.mjs`. The song's lead goes to voice 1, its bass to voice 2,
its chord and its percussion to voice 3, where a drum cuts the chord and the
chord comes back after it: the sequencer's rule for a chip with one voice for
both, `Sequencer.scheduleStep`.

## Known deviations

| What | Deliberate | Why | Affects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A write lands before the cycle it is stamped with is clocked; several on one cycle land in order | yes | the 6510 writes between SID cycles; the oracle's driver takes the same convention | when a register lands, to within one cycle |
| The combined waveforms are a model fitted to reSID-fp's tables, themselves fitted to kevtris's samplings of one 6581 R2 | yes | no samplings of our own; the model is the documented one, bits pulling on their neighbours | what a combined waveform's bits are on a chip other than that one |
| The noise register's reset by the test bit and the output's fade with no waveform take a warm 6581 R3's times | yes | both are a capacitor discharging and vary with the chip and its temperature; the oracle takes the same | how long a program has to hold the test bit; how long OSC3 reads the old value |
| The 8580 is not modelled | yes, for now | its digital part differs in the combined waveforms and a delay on the triangle and sawtooth, its analog part everywhere | the second half of the C64's life |

## Power-on state

`reset()` is the reset line: registers cleared, the noise register all ones
and clocked once, the pulse comparator high, the envelope in its release
phase with its rate register cleared. The accumulators keep their power-on
value, every even bit high, and the envelope counters theirs, 0xaa, which they
count down from at the fastest rate as soon as the chip is clocked; both are
what reSID-fp does and what its authors measured. The driver's power-on sets
the volume to full and the filter to nothing.
