# Commodore 64: MOS 6581 SID (`c64`)

<p align="center">
  <a href="c64.md">English</a> &bull;
  <a href="c64_ja.md">日本語</a>
</p>


The Commodore 64's sound: the 6581, three voices that are each an oscillator
with four waveforms that can be combined, an envelope with a gate, sync and
ring modulation between neighbours, and a filter they share. The method
behind every section is in [CONFORMANCE.md](../CONFORMANCE.md).

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Machine** | Commodore 64 (the PAL clock, 985248 Hz; NTSC machines ran at 1022727) |
| **Status** | **in progress**: the digital part is identical to reSID-fp on every voice's two values, the driver plays every role, the analog stage is a profile from the documents |
| **Core** | written from the documents (`packages/chipvoice/src/chips/c64/sid.ts`); the analog profile in `dsp.ts` |
| **Licence of the core** | MIT, like the rest of the package. reSID-fp, the oracle, is GPL and lives in the harness only: decision 18 |
| **Sheet updated** | 2026-09-27, by hand and by `conform` |

## Digital parity

Measured by [`conform`](../../packages/conform), the harness, against
[reSID-fp](../../packages/conform/oracles/residfp), on each voice's two
digital values - the twelve-bit waveform output and the eight-bit envelope
counter, read before the DACs - over three songs through the driver and ten
scripts in [`packages/conform/corpus/c64`](../../packages/conform/corpus/c64).
The numbers between the markers are written by the harness (`pnpm --filter
chipvoice-conform baseline:c64`); the reading of them below is a person's. CI
reruns the corpus and fails if the identical count falls below the committed
baseline.

**What the numbers say.** Every log is identical to reSID-fp on all six
streams: every waveform at every pitch and pulse width in the corpus, the four
combined waveforms and the noise combinations with their write-back into the
register, the noise at eight rates and its reset by the test bit, every
attack, decay and release rate, every sustain level, gates a few cycles apart
in every phase, the rate register set below its count mid-attack, sync and
ring modulation at four ratios including a synced source, the test bit on a
source, a source at zero, and the two songs through the driver. Two things the
first run found, both in the chip and both facts about the hardware rather
than the model: power-on is a reset, which clocks the noise register once as
the reset line goes; and rate 8 of the envelope is 392 cycles a step, one
more than the datasheet's 391, which is what the register value kevtris read
off the chip says. The combined waveforms are a model with six numbers per
combination, and the model matches reSID-fp's tables on all 4096 entries of
each: `pnpm --filter chipvoice-conform fit:c64` scores it.

Four scripts new in this sheet - `script-dense-sawtooth`, `script-dense-triangle`,
`script-dense-noise`, `script-dense-combined` - hold a dense
waveform on all three voices at once for the whole log, several seconds each,
rather than the pulses the rest of the corpus uses everywhere but the voice
under test: the twelve-bit output changes on nearly every cycle, on every
voice, at the same time. They are identical to reSID-fp too. They exist
because the harness used to hold every change of every stream in memory as
one object each, and three sawtoothed voices for eight seconds - three times
the length here - ran it out of memory; a compact `ChangeStream` (typed-array
columns, not objects) and a streaming compare fixed that (P7-11). That exact
case, three sawtooths for eight seconds, is not in this corpus: it still
takes about a minute and a gigabyte and a half on its own, next to a few
seconds for everything above combined, so it runs on demand instead, with
`pnpm --filter chipvoice-conform check:c64:dense`
(`packages/conform/corpus/c64-dense`) - also identical, not part of
`check:c64` or CI.

One script and one song are new for the filter (P7-9): `script-filter` writes
`$D415`-`$D418` directly, two voices sharing the filter while its resonance
and cutoff move and a third cycles its three modes; `song-filter` plays the
two arranger words that reach it, `lead: "sweep"` and `bass: "resonant"`.
Neither moves a digital trace - the filter sits after the DACs, entirely in
the analog stage below, which the corpus cannot measure - but both are
identical here on the six streams above, since routing a voice through the
filter or not does not change what it writes to `osc`/`env`. They exist so
the corpus carries a write to every register the driver reaches; what the
filter actually sounds like is still the unmeasured 6581 profile in "Analog
stage" below, not something this table can speak to.

**What the numbers say.** Every script that never selects a combined waveform
- the dense sawtooth, triangle and noise scripts, the noise, envelope,
sync/ring and single-waveform scripts, and both songs, which use plain
waveforms throughout - is still identical to reSID-fp's 8580, because nothing
in those logs exercises the one place the two models differ digitally that
this fit does not reach exactly: `script-combined` and `script-dense-combined`
diverge, both entirely inside the combined-waveform bits, at the score the
next section's fit predicts. This is not a bug to fix by hand-tuning further;
it is what fitting a six-number neighbour-pull model to a table reSID-fp's own
8580 code builds from a different, more detailed transistor model (its own
`WaveformCalculator.cpp`'s `config[1]` rows, cited but never copied here,
decision 41) looks like when the simpler model cannot reach every entry.

## Combined waveforms against a real 6581

The digital parity above matches reSID-fp's own table for the combined
waveforms, but that table is itself a fit to kevtris's samplings of one 6581
R2 (decision 18); a 100% score there is a 100% score against a fit, not a
second, independent chip. `libsidplayfp/combined-waveforms`
([docs/HARDWARE-EVIDENCE.md](../HARDWARE-EVIDENCE.md#c64-sid-6581-8580) has
the source and licence) publishes raw OSC3 samplings of named physical SID
units that this codebase has never seen. Fetch one 6581 R4AR's four files
with `pnpm --filter chipvoice-conform evidence:fetch c64`, then score the
model against them with `pnpm --filter chipvoice-conform evidence:c64:sheet`.
OSC3 exposes only the top eight of the waveform generator's twelve bits, so
that shift is applied before comparing, the same as the sampling project's
own scoring tool does.

**What this settles and what it cannot.** The model was fitted to reSID-fp's
table, not to this unit, so a mismatch here is not a regression - it is a
measurement of how much one real 6581 differs from another on this part of
the chip, which the sheet already says is more than any other chip here. Three
of the four combinations agree on three-quarters to five-sixths of the 4096
values with a real, independently sourced chip; the fourth, pulse+saw+triangle,
agrees on 94%. This is evidence about the digital waveform generator, before
the DACs - it does not move the analog stage below, which still has no
capture of a unit of our own.

## Test ROMs

Run by the harness on a 6510 it carries for the purpose (`pnpm --filter
chipvoice-conform roms:c64`), in CI on every push. VICE carries a set of test
programs for the SID (`testprogs/SID`, GPL-2, pinned at r46273) that read OSC3
and ENV3 back from a 6510 and report a verdict without the KERNAL, through
VICE's own debug cartridge convention: a byte at `$D7FF` (0 for pass, `$ff`
for fail) and the border colour at `$D020`, then a jump to itself. Fourteen of
them, across eight groups, are vendored under
[`packages/conform/roms/vice-sid`](../../packages/conform/roms/vice-sid) and
run on the minimal 6510, VIC-II raster line and CIA 1 timers the harness
carries for exactly this
([`packages/conform/src/roms/c64.mjs`](../../packages/conform/src/roms/c64.mjs)).
The rest need the KERNAL, a disk, an ear or a screen this harness cannot read,
are interactive, or depend on a warm-up run; the vendored readme says why for
each. None of the fourteen chosen programs are 8580-only, so none is "not
applicable" here, and this fixture still only builds a 6581: an 8580 run of
the same fourteen would need the fixture's own SID model wired to an option
the way `c64Chip.digital({ model })` already is, which nothing here does yet.

"verified: C64C+8580, C64+6581") - the same fifteen rates the formula tests

`busvalue` was the one genuine divergence the first run found, fixed since
(P2-1). It writes a write-only register, reads a read-only one (OSC3), then
reads a non-existent register, and expects the OSC3 value back. Real hardware
keeps an internal data bus latch that, the test's own readme says, is
"refreshed not only on writes but also on valid reads from the read-only
registers". `Sid.read()` (`packages/chipvoice/src/chips/c64/sid.ts`) used to
return OSC3 and ENV3 without touching that latch, so a later read of a
write-only or missing register gave the last written byte; it now leaves what
it read there. Nothing else exercised the gap: the driver never reads the SID
back, and the parity corpus never reads a missing register after a read-only
one, which is why it had not shown up against reSID-fp.

## The 8580

The second SID, the one in the second half of the C64's life (the 65 and the
C64C): a redesigned chip in a newer process, same registers, same envelope
generator, but a digital waveform generator whose combined-waveform bits pull
differently, a longer capacitor discharge on both the floating-waveform output
and the noise register's test-bit reset, near-linear DACs instead of the
6581's kinked ones, and its own filter. Every fact below is read from a
document or measured against the oracle's own tables, never from porting
reSID-fp's own 8580 source into this package (decision 41); each row below
names its source.

**Choosing it.** `model: "8580"` on `Chip.create`, `renderPerformance`,
`renderProject`, or a project's `settings.model`; anywhere `"6581"` (the
default) already works:

```js
const chip = await Chip.create(ctx, { chip: "c64", model: "8580" });
```

It threads through `ChipCreateOptions` to `SidProcessor`'s
`processorOptions.model` in the worklet and to `c64Chip.digital({ model })`
in the harness and in `RenderOptions`/`renderPerformance`'s own `model`
field; an unrecognised value falls back to the 6581. `SID_8580_PROFILE` and
`SID_6581_PROFILE` (`packages/chipvoice/src/chips/c64/dsp.ts`) are also
exported directly for anything that reads a profile without going through
`Chip.create`.

**Combined waveforms.** The same six-number neighbour-pull model as the
6581's, `COMBINED_8580` (`packages/chipvoice/src/chips/c64/sid.ts`), fitted
independently against reSID-fp's own 8580 tables
(`pnpm --filter chipvoice-conform fit:c64 -- --model 8580`) rather than
copied from reSID-fp's own 8580 `config[1]` rows in its
`WaveformCalculator.cpp` (decision 41: that file may be read to know what to
measure, never ported). A coordinate descent from many random starting
points, keeping the best per combination, since a single descent from the
6581's own numbers under-converged on three of the four:

| Combination | Match against reSID-fp's 8580 table |
| --- | --- |
| `saw+triangle` | 99.05 % (4057/4096) |
| `pulse+triangle` | 97.51 % (3994/4096) |
| `pulse+saw` | 95.00 % (3891/4096) |
| `pulse+saw+triangle` | 92.60 % (3793/4096) |

None reaches the 6581's 100%: reSID-fp's own 8580 code builds its table from
a different, more detailed transistor model than the simple bias/pull-per-bit
one this package uses for both chips, so a perfect fit is not expected here.
The digital-parity block above shows exactly where this shortfall surfaces -
`script-combined` and `script-dense-combined`, the only two logs that select
a combined waveform, at 98.98% and 91.19% - and nowhere else: every log that
never combines waveforms is still 100% identical. There is no equivalent of
the "against a real 6581" section above for the 8580: `libsidplayfp/combined-waveforms`
does sample 8580 units too, but fetching and scoring against one is not part
of this ticket; a real capture remains open for whoever measures the analog
stage (P7-8).

**Waveform timing.** Two capacitor-discharge times, both cited from
reSID-fp's `WaveformGenerator.cpp`/`.h`
(`FLOATING_OUTPUT_TTL_8580R5`/`SHIFT_REGISTER_RESET_8580R5`, next to the
6581R3 constants the existing model already used): a floating waveform
output (no waveform bit set) takes roughly ten times as long to settle to
zero on an 8580 as on a 6581, and the noise register's reset by the test bit
takes roughly thirteen times as long. Both are model-selected constants
(`FLOATING_TTL`/`NOISE_RESET` in `sid.ts`), not new formulas. Also from
`WaveformGenerator.h`: the 8580 has no accumulator pulldown on a sawtooth
combination (a 6581-only effect) and no swap-exclusion in the noise
write-back when the test bit releases (also 6581-only); both are gated on
`model` in `Oscillator.compute()`/`writeControl()`. None of these three
change what a corpus log without a combined waveform, a long silence or a
noise write-back looks like, which is consistent with every non-combined log
in the parity block above staying at 100%.

**OSC3's one-cycle lag.** `WaveformGenerator.h`'s `tri_saw_pipeline`: on the
8580, the value `$D41B` (OSC3) reads back is the sawtooth/triangle pipeline's
value from one cycle earlier than the waveform generator's own output that
cycle; the 6581 has no such pipeline and reads the current cycle. This is a
register read-back detail only - the `osc3` field the `Sid` class tracks
separately from `output` - and never changes the audio output itself, which
is why it does not show as a divergence anywhere but OSC3 in the
`script-combined`/`script-dense-combined` rows above (those two logs are also
the only ones whose OSC3 stream is a sawtooth or triangle at the time it
diverges).

**DACs.** `SID_8580_PROFILE.ladderRatio = 2.0` with `ladderTerminated: true`,
from reSID-fp's `Dac` documentation ("very accurately matched," no
discontinuities, ratio about 2.0): a terminated ladder at exactly that ratio
is a perfect binary one, so the 8580's twelve-bit DACs are (to the precision
this document gives) linear, unlike the 6581's unterminated 2.2 mismatch.
`test/c64-8580.mjs` checks the weights this profile produces against
`ladderWeights(12, 2, true)` bit for bit.

**Filter.** `SID_8580_PROFILE.cutoff`, `qLow` and `qHigh`, derived from plain
reSID's `filter.cc` (the same file, and the same named "CSG 8580R5 1690 25"
chip, NEXT-04 pointed at): unlike the 6581's kinked curve, `filter.cc`'s own
fixed-point expression for the 8580 is an exact line, `w0 = 82355 * (fc + 1)
>> 11`, which is `Hz = 12500 * (fc + 1) / 2048`; at the register's two
endpoints that is 6.1035 Hz at `fc = 0` and 12500 Hz at `fc = 2047`, the
`cutoff` table's two points. `qLow`/`qHigh` (0.7072/2.5924) come the same
way, from `filter.cc`'s `_1024_div_Q_table`'s first and last fixed-point
entries, cross-checked against the closed-form `1/Q` the same file computes
them from. This is `filter.cc`'s own published table used as measurement
data (decision 41 again: read, not ported - no filter code was copied, only
the two numbers its table implies at each end), which is what NEXT-04 asked
for. It is not a capture of a real 8580's line-out, and the op-amp's shape
between the two measured corners (the 6581's curve has a kink at 1024 partway
through; whether the 8580's is that simple a line in practice or only in
`filter.cc`'s fixed-point formula is not verified here) is still open,
alongside the same P7-8 capture the 6581 needs.

**What stays unmeasured.** No line-out capture of a real 8580 of our own,
same as the 6581. No independent hardware check of the combined waveforms
against a named physical 8580, the way the section above checks the 6581
against `libsidplayfp/combined-waveforms`' 6581R4AR_3789_14. Whether the
filter's shape between its two measured corners is really the plain line
`filter.cc`'s fixed-point formula gives, or (like the 6581) has a kink
`filter.cc` does not encode for this chip. And the ADSR tables, the noise
LFSR taps and the gate/sync/ring-modulation logic: `EnvelopeGenerator.cpp`
has no 8580-specific branch in reSID-fp, so this package makes none either,
carrying the 6581 section's envelope and sync/ring-mod facts over unchanged;
this has not been independently checked against an 8580-specific document.

## PSID/RSID playback

`importPsid`/`renderPsid` (`packages/chipvoice/src/psid-import.ts`) play a
PSID or RSID file - HVSC's `SID_file_format.txt` - by actually running its
INIT and PLAY machine code, on a 6510 written from the documents
(`packages/chipvoice/src/chips/c64/cpu6510.ts`) and a minimal, disclosed C64
environment, then return every write the tune made to the SID as a
`PerformancePlan`, the same shape `importVgm` returns: rendered through
`renderPerformance(plan, c64Chip, {model: plan.model, clockHz:
plan.clockHz})`, or in one call through `renderPsid`. `psid-import.ts` stays
top-level, like `vgm-import.ts`; the CPU lives under `chips/c64/` like every
other chip's own core. Neither is on `scores/mixing/calibrate.mjs`'s own
call path (calibration only drives a chip's note-level `ChipCore`, never
this import/CPU path), so neither adds weight to its engine hash.

Conformance rests on three legs, none of them shipped or ported GPL code
(decision 41): a hand-written unit suite against the public opcode/cycle
tables and 6502.org's own decimal-mode document; Klaus Dormann's and Bruce
Clark's own self-checking 6502 test programs, vendored as private conformance
tooling in `packages/conform` and run against this same `Cpu6510`; and,
since this ticket, an independent oracle - libsidplayfp itself, cloned and
built at a pinned revision into a gitignored `.artifacts/` directory
(`scores/psid-corpus/native-oracle.mjs`, the same pinned-clone pattern
`scores/arrangements/native-oracle.py` uses for Game_Music_Emu), never
vendored and never shipped in `packages/chipvoice`.

The oracle's own SID-write trace, plugged in through libsidplayfp's public
`SidConfig::sidEmulation` hook rather than any patched-in logging, settled
INIT's calling convention empirically instead of leaving it inferred: `A`
(the song number, zero-based) and `P` (0x24 before `PHP`) match libsidplayfp's
own reference driver exactly. `X` and `Y` do not, and are now confirmed
genuinely undefined - not just by the file format spec's own silence, but by
libsidplayfp's own driver too, which leaves them holding whatever an
unrelated CIA/raster setup branch last loaded, not a documented value; this
package's own choice to zero them stays a deliberate, spec-neutral default,
not a guess at an undocumented convention. See "Known limits" and the corpus
table below.

The same oracle also caught a real conformance gap, since fixed: `Cpu6510`'s
read-modify-write instructions (`INC`/`DEC`/`ASL`/`LSR`/`ROL`/`ROR`, and the
illegal SLO/RLA/SRE/RRA/DCP/ISC combinations) were missing the NMOS 6502's
documented dummy write - on real hardware, an RMW instruction writes the
unmodified value back to the bus once before it writes the modified one, two
writes one cycle apart, not one - which some real PSID/RSID tunes lean on
deliberately (an `INC`/`ASL` straight on a SID register for a phantom gate
retrigger). `Cpu6510` only ever performed the second write; it now performs
both, verified against the oracle's own cycle-exact trace.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Core** | `chips/c64/cpu6510.ts`, the 6510 CPU; `psid-import.ts`, the header, the environment and the public API |
| **Conformance** | `test/cpu6510.mjs`: every documented opcode's result, flags and cycle count, including page-cross penalties; the NMOS decimal-mode ADC/SBC algorithm and RMW's own dummy write; every stable illegal opcode a real SID tune is known to use; every unstable-illegal and JAM/KIL opcode, rejected by name rather than guessed at or hung on. `test/psid-import.mjs`: header parsing, the named rejections below, PAL/NTSC and 6581/8580 selection, and an end-to-end render. `packages/conform`'s `check:6510`: Klaus Dormann's 6502 functional test and Bruce Clark's decimal test, a second, independent proof. `scores/psid-corpus`: the libsidplayfp oracle above |

**What this environment models.** One SID at `$D400`. CIA 1 timer A as a
60 Hz IRQ source, with the documented "writing the high byte loads the
counter" quirk. The VIC as a once-a-frame raster IRQ pulse, at the file
format's own PAL (19656 cycles) or NTSC (17045 cycles) period, plus its own
real badline DMA steal: once per raster line in `$30`-`$F7` whose low 3 bits
match `$D011`'s own YSCROLL, with DEN (bit 4) set, the VIC steals the bus for
43 cycles (`BADLINE_STEAL_CYCLES`), at the real trigger point 11 cycles into
that line (`VICII_FETCH_CYCLE`) - both measured directly against
libsidplayfp's own cycle-exact VIC-II core (`c64/VIC_II/mos656x.h`/`.cpp` in
the pinned oracle revision) by instrumenting its own BA pin and its own
`startBadline()`, not read off a datasheet's rounded "40 cycles" figure. The
file format's own default environment for both kinds of file: for PSID, the
VIC raster IRQ enabled only when the speed flag is 0, CIA 1 timer A always
running but IRQ-active only when the speed flag is 1; for RSID, CIA 1 timer A
running and already IRQ-active, the VIC raster set up but left disabled,
since an RSID tune installs and manages its own handlers. Either way,
`$D011` starts at `$1B` (DEN set, YSCROLL 3) - what the KERNAL this
environment never ships always leaves it at by the time INIT runs on real
hardware, and what libsidplayfp's own reference driver (`psiddrv.a65`'s
`vicinit`) pokes explicitly for the same reason. The bank register `$01`,
written with the file format's own formula before every INIT/PLAY call for a
PSID (an RSID manages it itself, once its own handler is in). A PSID with a
nonzero `playAddress` gets a small environment-installed trampoline at
`$0334` that acknowledges both modeled IRQ sources before calling PLAY,
because either one can trigger the same handler; a PSID with `playAddress`
0, and every RSID, keeps whatever handler the tune's own INIT installed
instead. After INIT returns, the CPU is parked in a `JMP`-to-itself idle loop
at `$0350` - what real hardware does between IRQs - rather than left to fall
into whatever happens to be at the return address.

**What it does not model, and rejects by name rather than guessing at.**
Multi-SID files (a v3/v4 header naming a second or third SID address).
Compute!'s Sidplayer MUS format (flag bit 0) - only the built-in
machine-code player is supported. An RSID file that needs the C64 BASIC
interpreter (flag bit 1) - no BASIC interpreter is modeled. All three raise a
named `PsidFormatError` rather than a silent fallback. Left out with no
error, because nothing observable in a played-back tune depends on them: CIA
1's timer B, TOD clock and serial port; sprite DMA and every other VIC-II
raster-comparator behaviour besides the once-a-frame IRQ pulse and the
badline steal above (no mid-frame `$D011`/`$D012` writes changing the raster
IRQ's own target line, and no sprite-driven bus steals); bank switching and
KERNAL/BASIC ROM images - this package ships no C64 ROM image and never will
(decision 41), so `$01` is written with the formula's value but nothing
reads it back to change what memory decodes to; and a live, cycle-stepped
SID read-back - `$D41B`/`$D41C` (OSC3/ENV3) return the last byte written to
the SID bus during import, the same fallback `Sid.ts` already uses for every
other register, not a simulated oscillator or envelope counter.

### Oracle: libsidplayfp

`scores/psid-corpus` scores two small, self-authored PSID fixtures
(CC0, this ticket's own - never HVSC or a commercial rip, decision 41)
against libsidplayfp, cloned and built at a pinned revision into a
gitignored `.artifacts/` directory
(`native-oracle.mjs`; GPL-2.0-or-later, never vendored). A small,
purpose-written logger (`sidplayfp-harness.cpp`) is plugged into
libsidplayfp's own CPU, CIA, VIC and tune-loading engine through its public
`SidConfig::sidEmulation` hook - no SID audio is emulated, and no libsidplayfp
source is patched - and prints every SID write with its exact clock cycle,
the same `{cycle, addr, value}` shape `psid-import.ts`'s own environment
records:

- **`convention-probe.sid`**: INIT stores `A`, `X`, `Y` and the PHP-pulled `P`
  straight to `$D400`-`$D403`, once, before doing anything else - the
  calling convention itself, read back as a write-stream. `X` and `Y` are
  excluded from the comparison entirely (`compare.mjs`'s own `ignoreAddrs`):
  a spec-undefined register has no defined answer to score against, so
  those two writes are never counted as matched or as a divergence, and the
  scan carries on past them instead of stopping there. The "INIT registers"

## Power-on state

`reset()` is the reset line: registers cleared, the noise register all ones
and clocked once, the pulse comparator high, the envelope in its release
phase with its rate register cleared. The accumulators keep their power-on
value, every even bit high, and the envelope counters theirs, 0xaa, which they
count down from at the fastest rate as soon as the chip is clocked; both are
what reSID-fp does and what its authors measured. The driver's power-on sets
the volume to full and the filter to nothing.
